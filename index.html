The Movement Vocabulary
Eleven patterns. What they are. Why they matter. What breaks when they're missing.
The body is one instrument. The framework makes sure you're playing all the strings, not just the loud ones.
The human body does not recognize muscle groups. It has never heard of chest day. It does not know what a bro-split is, and it would not be impressed if you explained one. What the body recognizes are patterns of force — pushing, pulling, hinging, bracing, rotating, landing, carrying. These eleven patterns represent the minimum vocabulary needed to describe everything a human body does.
1. Push
What It Is
Any movement where your body produces force to move a load away from your center of mass, primarily through the chest, shoulders, and triceps. Horizontal pressing, vertical pressing, and explosive extension all qualify.
Real-World Anchor
Pushing a stalled car. Getting up off the floor. Throwing a punch. Pushing a stroller uphill. Pressing a child overhead to make them laugh.
What Happens If It's Missing
Push is rarely absent — it is the most naturally over-represented pattern in gym culture. The risk is dominance, not absence. When push chronically outpaces pull, the shoulders internally rotate, the upper back weakens, and injury risk climbs.
2. Pull
What It Is
Any movement where your body produces force to move a load toward your center of mass, primarily through the lats, rhomboids, rear delts, and biceps. Horizontal pulling (rows, face pulls), vertical pulling (pull-ups), and scapular retraction all count.
Real-World Anchor
Opening a heavy door. Pulling a child onto your hip. Climbing a rope or fence. Rowing a kayak. Hauling a suitcase off an overhead bin.
What Happens If It's Missing
Shoulder impingement, rotator cuff irritation, postural collapse, reduced punching power. The retraction phase of every punch combination is a pull. Faster scapular retraction means faster combinations. This is the corrective priority.
3. Hinge
What It Is
Any movement where the primary action is bending at the hips while maintaining a neutral spine, loading the posterior chain — hamstrings, glutes, and spinal erectors. Deadlifts, kettlebell swings, good mornings, Romanian deadlifts.
Real-World Anchor
Picking up a toddler off the floor. Lifting a suitcase. Shoveling snow. This is the most frequently performed movement pattern in daily life and the one most commonly injured when untrained.
What Happens If It's Missing
Hamstring strains, lower back vulnerability, lumbar overload. Without a trained hinge, the body defaults to spinal flexion under load — the mechanism behind the majority of non-traumatic lower back injuries.
4. Squat
What It Is
Any movement where the primary action is bending at both the knees and hips simultaneously to lower and raise the center of mass. Bodyweight squats, goblet squats, jump squats, thrusters. Knee-dominant bilateral lower body.
Real-World Anchor
Sitting down and standing up. Getting in and out of a car. Dropping to the floor to play with your kids and getting back up. The inability to squat is the beginning of the end of physical autonomy.
What Happens If It's Missing
Loss of ankle and hip mobility, quad atrophy, reduced impact absorption, eventual inability to get up from the floor without assistance. The sit-rise test is one of the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality.
5. Rotation
What It Is
Any movement where the trunk produces or resists rotational force. Slam balls, woodchops, throwing, Pallof presses, offset carries. The obliques, transverse abdominis, and hip rotators drive or brake the movement.
Real-World Anchor
Throwing a ball with your kids. Turning to check a blind spot. Every punch ever thrown. Rotation is the most athletic pattern — almost every sport expresses power through trunk rotation.
What Happens If It's Missing
Reduced power output in striking and throwing. Lower back vulnerability from compensating with lumbar rotation instead of thoracic. Rotational power is one of the first capacities to decline after 40.
6. Core / Anti-Extension
What It Is
Any movement where the trunk resists forces that want to bend, twist, extend, or collapse the spine. Planks, dead bugs, loaded carries, bracing under heavy compound lifts. The defining feature is stability, not motion.
Real-World Anchor
Carrying a heavy object without leaning. Maintaining posture under fatigue. Holding a child on one hip while opening a door. Core stability keeps you upright when external forces try to move you in directions you don't want to go.
What Happens If It's Missing
Lower back pain, postural collapse under load, reduced force transfer in every compound movement. A weak core creates a leak in the kinetic chain that makes everything else less efficient and more dangerous.
7. Locomotion
What It Is
Any movement where your body travels through space under its own power. Running, walking, shadowboxing footwork, crawling, shuffling, sprinting, sled pushing. Your body starts in one place and ends in another.
Real-World Anchor
Running after a child who has bolted toward a street. Hiking with your family. Humans are endurance locomotion specialists — designed to travel distances.
What Happens If It's Missing
Cardiovascular deconditioning, loss of gait efficiency, reduced ankle and hip mobility. A body that can deadlift 300 pounds but cannot run a mile has traded its most fundamental capability for a narrow one.
8. Plyometric / Power
What It Is
Any movement where the goal is maximum force production in minimum time. Jump squats, explosive step-ups, slam balls, sprint starts. Power is strength expressed at speed.
Real-World Anchor
Catching yourself when you trip. Reacting to a child falling. After 40, fast-twitch fibers decline at roughly double the rate of slow-twitch. This is the pattern most likely to prevent a fall from becoming a fracture.
What Happens If It's Missing
Loss of explosive capacity, increased fall risk, reduced ability to react to sudden demands. The difference between a 44-year-old who moves like an athlete and one who moves like a retiree often comes down to power.
9. Mobility
What It Is
The ability to move a joint through its full range of motion under muscular control. Not flexibility (passive range) — mobility is range you own and can produce force through. Walkouts, thoracic rotations, wall slides, hip flexor stretches.
Real-World Anchor
Reaching a high shelf without pain. Looking over your shoulder to reverse a car. Playing on the floor with your children and standing back up fluidly.
What Happens If It's Missing
Compensation patterns cascade. When the thoracic spine cannot rotate, the lumbar spine tries to. When the hips cannot flex fully, the lower back rounds. Every mobility deficit creates a compensation, and every compensation becomes an injury waiting for a trigger.
10. Single-Leg
What It Is
Any movement where one leg bears the primary load. Lunges, split squats, step-ups, single-leg deadlifts. Walking is technically single-leg. Every asymmetry your bilateral squats hide, single-leg work exposes.
Real-World Anchor
Walking up stairs. Stepping over a baby gate. Pivoting in the ring. Running is a continuous single-leg movement. Unilateral lower body strength and balance are among the strongest predictors of functional longevity.
What Happens If It's Missing
Undetected side-to-side imbalances, reduced balance and proprioception, increased ACL and meniscus risk from lateral instability. Falls.
11. Carry
What It Is
Any movement where you hold a load and walk. Farmer's walks, suitcase carries, overhead carries. Loaded locomotion combining grip endurance, trunk stability, and gait mechanics under external load.
Real-World Anchor
Carrying groceries from the car in one trip. Walking through an airport with luggage. Carrying a sleeping child up the stairs. Arguably the most universally functional pattern and the one almost no traditional program includes.
What Happens If It's Missing
Grip failure under sustained demand, trunk instability under asymmetric loading. For a boxer, grip endurance affects fist integrity in later rounds. For a father, it determines whether you can carry a sleeping 40-lb child from the car to the bed without waking them.

Why DECADs, Not Weeks
319 days do not divide cleanly into weeks. They divide into 31 DECADs of 10 days each, plus a 9-day closing stretch. The DECAD is the natural planning unit — long enough to cover all eleven patterns with honest frequency, short enough to course-correct before gaps compound, and mathematically clean against the 319-day arc.
A 7-day week gives you limited room to recover and still hit every corrective target. A 10-day cycle gives you three additional sessions to distribute across the patterns that need the most attention. The hinge corrective that needs three sessions per cycle can get a dedicated day and two supplementary appearances. The carry pattern that needs two sessions has room to breathe. Eccentric tendon work — which requires recovery days between sessions — can be spaced properly instead of crammed.
DECAD 1 runs Days 1–10. DECAD 2 runs Days 11–20. The math carries all the way to DECAD 31 (Days 301–310), followed by a 9-day closing period (Days 311–319) that operates as a compressed final cycle.
The DECAD is the unit that matters. The day is just a vote.

Substitution Logic
No kettlebell? No bands? Low energy? Under 15 minutes? Adapt without losing the pattern.
Training plans do not survive contact with life. Here is how to adapt when equipment, energy, or time is not what the template assumes.
If You Have No Kettlebell
Replace KB Swing with Good Mornings (bodyweight, higher reps) or Dumbbell Deadlift (heavier, lower reps). The hinge pattern stays. The tool changes.
If You Have No Bands
Replace Band Pull-Apart with bodyweight variation (arms extended, squeeze shoulder blades). Replace Face Pull with Inverted Row at a high angle. Replace Band External Rotation with side-lying dumbbell external rotation at light weight.
If Energy Is Low
Drop to the Minimum Viable Session for whatever day type you are on. Every template has one. The minimum viable session is not a consolation prize — it is the designed floor that still serves the DECAD's pattern integrity.
If Time Is Under 15 Minutes
Option 1: DB Thruster (squat + push) + Inverted Row (pull) + Farmer's Walk (carry + core + locomotion) = 5 patterns.
Option 2: KB Swing (hinge + power) + Pull-Up (pull) + Open-Book Rotation (mobility) = 4 patterns.
Option 3: Shadow Boxing (push + rotation + locomotion + power) + Band Pull-Apart (pull) + Wall Slides (mobility) = 6 patterns.
If You Are Training Outside (No Equipment)
Running (locomotion) + Bodyweight Squats (squat) + Walking Lunges (single-leg) + Push-Ups (push) + Bear Crawl (locomotion + core) = 5 patterns with nothing but the ground and gravity. Add a jog back with a heavy rock for carry and hinge.

What 319 Days Will Build
The full promise. Body, mind, spirit — every phase. Read this on the nights you can't push.
Read this on the nights you can't push. Let it pull.
Everything in this document is grounded in the system you've already built. The corrective protocols, the pattern architecture, the nutrition phases, the running progression, the appraisal dimensions — these aren't aspirations. They're infrastructure. Three domains. Every phase. Body, Mind, Spirit. Because this was never just about fitness.
Days 1–30 — The Foundation Pour
You're here. Ten days in, already deeper than most people get in a year of training. This phase is about establishing the neural pathways, the movement vocabulary, and the daily practice that everything else is built on.
The Body
Corrective exercises become familiar. The pull-up, inverted row, good morning, kettlebell swing, farmer's walk, foam roller, step-downs — these stop being "prescribed" and start being yours. Your body begins to recognize the difference between the patterns.
Thoracic spine begins to unlock. You'll feel this before you see it — less stiffness getting out of bed, less lower back compensating during rotation. The audible cracks on the foam roller start to quiet as the segments regain individual mobility.
Shoulder posture begins to shift. Twenty years of forward pull doesn't reverse in thirty days, but the 2:1 pull-to-push ratio is actively remodeling the force balance around your shoulder girdle.
Running base establishes. From 1.6 miles to approximately 15–18 cumulative miles by end of month. The eccentric heel drops are fortifying the Achilles, the pre-run activation circuit is teaching the lateral chain to fire.
Weight trends toward 212–214 lbs. The Race Fuel phase holds you in a mild deficit of ~380 cal/day. The AM readings tightening, the swings narrowing, the data telling a story of a body beginning to respond.
The Clean Fuel Effect
No alcohol, no processed sugar, no over-indulgence. Within the first two weeks, your sleep architecture begins to rebuild. Facial bloating begins to recede. Blood sugar stabilizes. Gut inflammation quiets. These changes are happening underneath the training, accelerating every other adaptation.
The Mind
Honest self-assessment becomes automatic. By Day 30 you've scored yourself across 11 dimensions thirty times. You can no longer lie to yourself about a session's quality.
Compliance-driven thinking replaces inspiration-driven thinking. You don't wait to feel like training. You look at what the system needs and you serve it.
The Spirit
The streak becomes identity. Thirty consecutive days through travel, snow days, sick kids, four hours of sleep, hotel gyms, living room floors — rewires your self-concept. You're no longer the man who starts and stops. You're the man who doesn't miss.
By Day 30 you won't look different. You'll move different. And you'll think different. That's the floor being poured.
Days 31–63 — The Proving Ground
The Jersey City Half Marathon is Day 63. Everything you've built gets stress-tested against an actual performance goal.
The Body
Running volume peaks at 20–25 miles/week in the final training weeks before taper. Your body has adapted from a 1.6-mile first run to sustained efforts of 8–10 miles.
Race day weight of 210–212 lbs is achievable. Eccentric tendon resilience is measurable. Hinge pattern is automatic. Three-plus sessions per decad for six consecutive decads means 18+ dedicated hinge sessions.
You run 13.1 miles on April 19th. Not a maybe. A will.
The Clean Fuel Effect
Sixty days without alcohol. Resting heart rate has dropped 3–7 BPM, directly improving running economy. Your body accesses stored fat more efficiently during training. Your skin has changed — complexion clears, under-eye darkness fades.
The Mind
You've scored yourself 63 times. The appraisal composite average has climbed from the mid-60s toward 72–75. The first Monthly Synthesis reveals patterns invisible at the daily level.
Race preparation teaches pacing — in training and in life. Not every day needs to be a peak. Some days are mile 4, steady, unremarkable, essential.
The Spirit
Crossing a finish line at 44 after starting from 1.6 miles eight weeks earlier is a future memory made real. The encyclopedia hits 80–100 exercises documented. Sixty-three consecutive days. The streak is no longer fragile. It's load-bearing.
Day 63 you cross a finish line. But the race was won in the basement on the nights you almost didn't show up.
Days 64–120 — The Cut
Phase 2 of the Nutrition Architecture activates: The Cut. The performance goal is behind you. Now the system pivots to aggressive, sustainable body recomposition.
The Body
Weight drops from ~210 toward 200–205 lbs. The muscle you've been building and protecting through Race Fuel stays. The fat that's been insulating it goes.
All five original corrective imbalances are functionally resolved. The hinge pattern is no longer corrective — it's just how you train. Visible muscle definition emerges in places your body hasn't recruited in decades.
Ground Zero and The Metronome evolve. You may create new circuits. The creative act of designing your own training is part of the Athletic Generalism identity.
The Clean Fuel Effect
Four months clean. The metabolic transformation is now structural, not just behavioral. Fasting glucose is lower. Triglycerides have dropped. HDL cholesterol has risen. Cognitive performance is at a level you haven't experienced in years. The cravings are gone. Not managed. Gone.
The Mind
The appraisal composite average pushes toward 78–82. Nutrition compliance is habitual. You're no longer tracking to build the habit. You're tracking because the data is interesting.
The Spirit
You pass 100 consecutive days. Then 120. The streak is no longer something you protect. It's something that protects you. Your kids have watched you do this for four months. They understand that Dad does this. Every day. No exceptions.
By Day 120, the man who couldn't keep a streak alive has kept one alive for four months straight. The identity isn't aspirational. It's proven.
Days 121–210 — The Long Middle
The novelty is gone. The race is months behind you. The initial excitement has been replaced by something quieter and more powerful: routine mastery.
The Body
Weight approaches 195–200 lbs. You're down 15–20 lbs from your starting weight with dramatically different body composition. Athletic Generalism becomes visible capability. You can deadlift, carry heavy loads, run 10 miles, throw rotational power, hold a plank, absorb impact, and move laterally — all in the same week, all without injury.
Thoracic spine is functionally mobile. Running is effortless at paces that used to be hard. A 9:37 pace that was your ceiling in February is now your recovery jog.
The Clean Fuel Effect
Seven months clean. Your relationship with food has been completely rewritten. You eat to fuel, not to soothe. The emotional eating patterns that were tangled up with stress, depletion, and the end-of-day crash have been replaced by a system. The financial savings are real and accumulating.
The Mind
The appraisal composite pushes into the mid-80s. You think in patterns. Not just movement patterns. You see your work week as a pattern architecture. You see your marriage as a compliance tracker. Decision fatigue around training is gone.
The Spirit
Two hundred consecutive days. You have done something that most human beings will never do in any domain. The future memories are becoming present memories. You're already more physically capable than you were six months ago.
By Day 210, people will ask what program you're on. The answer is: my own. Built from scratch. Every single day. For seven months.
Days 211–319 — The Final Hundred
Phase 3 of the Nutrition Architecture: Hold & Perform. The cut is complete or nearing completion. The final hundred days aren't about building. They're about proving what was built.
The Body
Target physique: 185–190 lbs, sub-11% body fat, at 5'10", age 44. That's a 28–33 lb transformation from your Day 1 starting point of 218 lbs. Not through starvation. Not through overtraining. Through 319 days of systematic, intelligent, honest work.
Every movement pattern is strong. Pull-ups in sets of 10+. Deadlifts at bodyweight or above. Carries at 70+ lbs per hand for distance. Injury resilience is fundamentally different. Running is a permanent capability.
The Clean Fuel Effect
Ten months clean. You've exited the cycle permanently. The shame-to-ego pendulum that once defined your relationship with your body has no entry point. Your bloodwork on December 31st will tell the story in clinical language. But the story you feel is simpler. You feel like the man you were supposed to become.
The model you've set for your children is complete. Not "Dad doesn't drink." Not "Dad eats healthy." Something deeper: Dad chose what went into his body with the same care and intentionality he chose how he moved it.
The Mind
The appraisal system has produced 319 data points across 11 dimensions. 3,509 individual scores. You've written a book. Not in the abstract "someday I'll write" sense. In the literal sense. 319 daily entries. 31 decad roundups. 10 monthly syntheses. 150+ exercise encyclopedia entries.
The Spirit
Three hundred and nineteen consecutive days. Not a single miss. Through winter storms, summer heat, school years, holidays, sick days, travel, family emergencies, work crises, and the ordinary, grinding, beautiful, exhausting days that make up the vast majority of a life.
The athlete is back. Not the boxer. Not the soldier. Not the man who defined himself by one sport or one era. A complete mover. An Athletic Generalist.
Your children watched. For 319 days, they watched their father show up. Not always strong. Not always inspired. But always there. Always honest about it. Always willing to do the minimum when the maximum wasn't available. They don't know the word "consistency." They know the shape of it — and it looks like their father, every morning, doing the thing.
You didn't just train for 319 days. You became the man who trains every day. That's not a resolution. That's a renovation.
